## EA vs NePS on Ackley Function — Observations

### Search Space Exploration

The EA (with Gaussian mutation, Tournament selection, Intermediate
recombination) concentrates its search tightly around a small region
near (0, 0) very quickly. This is because selection pressure and
recombination push the population toward the best-seen region early,
causing the search to exploit rather than explore. A few outlier points
(yellow, high fitness) represent the initial random population before
convergence. The EA never revisits the far corners of [-30, 30] after
the first generation.

NePS (Bayesian Optimization) shows a much broader initial exploration —
points are spread across the entire [-30, 30] search space in the early
iterations. This is because NePS begins with random sampling to build
an initial model, then uses that model to focus on promising regions.
This exploration-then-exploitation pattern is visible in the scatter
plot: yellow dots (bad configs) at the edges, dark purple dots (good
configs) near the centre.

### Convergence Speed

The EA converges very rapidly — the convergence curve drops steeply in
the first ~30 children evaluations and then flattens almost completely.
This shows the EA is good at fast initial improvement but gets stuck
once the population has converged to a local region. The final fitness
achieved is approximately 0.3 — close to but not exactly the true
optimum of 0.0.

NePS converges more gradually but more reliably. After a quick drop in the first three iterations (random lucky samples), the fitness keeps getting better until iteration 200, when it finally reaches a final value close to 0.0, which is the true global optimum of the Ackley function.

### Exploration vs Exploitation

The EA struggles to balance exploration and exploitation. Once the
population clusters near a good region, Gaussian mutation with sigma=1.0
only makes small local perturbations, preventing the algorithm from
escaping to find potentially better areas. Running the EA multiple times
with different random seeds shows significant variance in the final
result — sometimes it converges near 0.0, sometimes near 0.5, depending
on the initial population.

NePS maintains better exploration throughout — its surrogate model
actively identifies uncertain regions worth exploring and balances
this against exploiting the known good region near (0, 0). This leads
to a more consistent and reliable convergence to the true optimum
across multiple runs.

### Summary

For a simple 2D problem like Ackley, both methods find good solutions.
However NePS reaches the true optimum (≈0.0) more reliably while the
EA tends to stall slightly above it (≈0.3). For higher-dimensional or
more complex problems, this gap would likely grow significantly, since
the EA's tendency to converge prematurely becomes more problematic as
the search space grows.
